## Standing rules extracted

All are in [slide-design.md](../../references/slide-design.md) and the rubric.

1. **A slide leads with its claim.** The box goes under the headline whether it
   states the claim or qualifies it; the evidence follows.
2. **Three elements at most**: headline, lead box, one supporting block.
3. **A caveat lives inside the element it qualifies, in that element's own type.**
   Never in note type underneath. A guard set small is a guard nobody reads, and
   deleting it as clutter changes what the slide claims.
4. **Teaching content belongs in the voice.** Check the script before cutting a
   line from a slide: if it is already spoken, the slide loses nothing.
5. **A closing qualifier builds last**, rather than sitting on screen before what
   it qualifies.
6. **Highlight the term the slide teaches**, through one mechanism. A highlighted
   sentence is a highlighted nothing.
7. **Same job, same component** — verified by reading computed styles back, not
   by eye.
8. **A status gets its own label**, not an appended heading.
9. **Nothing resizes on interaction**, not only the quiz.
