Forty-seven countries have adhered to the same definition of an AI system. The OECD wrote it in 2019 and revised it in 2024. The EU copied it almost word for word into Article 3(1) of the AI Act. Agreement stops there.

The EU sets risk tiers in law, with fines reaching 7% of worldwide turnover. Singapore and the UK hand the job to the regulators that already exist, and publish guidance rather than rules. India says the same thing about philosophy, but has binding rules on labelling synthetic content in force since February 2026. Korea passed a framework act, in force January 2026. China has no comprehensive AI law, and binding rules on generative AI and content labelling that it already enforces. The United States reversed direction in 2025 and is now litigating against its own states.

THE QUESTION THAT HAS AN ANSWER

Ask a better question.

“Which of my uses, where, judged by whom?”

does have an answer

So "is AI regulated?" has no answer. The word covers a Korean statute, a Chinese labelling rule, a Singapore guideline and an American lawsuit against a state. "Which of my uses, where, judged by whom?" does have an answer, and you can work it out in an afternoon. That question is the shape of everything that follows.
