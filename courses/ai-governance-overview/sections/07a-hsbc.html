Deputy Commissioner for Personal Data Protection · Re HSBC Bank (Singapore) [2021] SGPDPC 3

One case in this course ended well for the company. In 2021 a rejected credit-card applicant asked HSBC Singapore for the algorithmically generated scores behind the refusal. HSBC declined. Singapore's Deputy Commissioner agreed that the bank was entitled to decline, because the scores were evaluative opinion data. The law settled that. Then he went further than he needed to. He said that HSBC had acted reasonably by providing information about how it uses data and technology to conduct credit facility assessments, and that from the perspective of accountability and disclosure of policies and practices, HSBC had acquitted itself.

WHAT HE WAS POINTING AT

Two documents, published in advance.

A Principle for the Ethical Use of Big Data and AI

Published by HSBC before the complaint was made.

A Credit Decisioning Policy Statement

How data and technology are used to assess credit.

The publications did not win the case. They let a regulator satisfy itself quickly that the organisation was in control.
He was pointing at two documents that HSBC had published: a Principle for the Ethical Use of Big Data and AI, and a Credit Decisioning Policy Statement. The publications did not win the case. What they did was let a regulator satisfy itself quickly that the organisation was in control.
